# Measurement Support Map $s(p)$ Report

## 1. Concept and Definition
The **Measurement Support Map** $s(p)$ quantifies how strongly the actual Sentinel-2 low-resolution observation limits or constrains the structural hypothesis proposed by the super-resolution reconstruction at a specific physical coordinate.

### Mathematical Definition
$$ s(p) = \exp\left(-\frac{e(p)^2}{2\sigma_b^2}\right) $$
Where:
- $e = |y - \hat{y}|$ is the measurement residual calculated via the Effective Observation Operator $\hat{D}$.
- $\sigma_b$ is the operator/reconstruction uncertainty standard deviation.

### Strict Interpretation (What $s(p)$ is NOT)
- $s(p)$ is **NOT** a probability of correctness.
- $s(p)$ is **NOT** a probability of hallucination.
- A value of $s(p) \approx 1.0$ simply denotes that the real 10m measurement perfectly constrains the 2.5m local structure.
- A value of $s(p) \approx 0.0$ signifies the 10m measurement lacks sufficient information to constrain the 2.5m structural hypothesis generated by the SR network.

## 2. Relationship to $\hat{D}$ and the Residual $e$
The Support Map is structurally downstream from the Effective Observation Operator $\hat{D}$:
1. **Prediction**: $\hat{D}$ generates $\hat{y}$ (the expected observation at 10m).
2. **Residual**: $e$ quantifies the literal deviation from reality ($y - \hat{y}$) on the 10m grid.
3. **Support**: $s(p)$ transforms this residual into a normalized constraint score bounded to $[0, 1]$. 

## 3. Spatial Mapping (10m to 2.5m)
By definition, $e$ exists on the 10m sampling grid because the Sentinel-2 physical instrument measures at 10m. However, the evaluation of constraint must happen on the 2.5m analysis grid. 

To achieve this, the 10m $s_{lr}$ map is explicitly upsampled to the 2.5m HR grid using a nearest-neighbor (Kronecker-like) broadcast (`numpy.repeat`). This guarantees that a single 10m physical pixel formally and identically constrains the $4 \times 4$ block of 2.5m reconstructed pixels nestled physically within its footprint.

## 4. Uncertainty Treatment
The operator's base uncertainty $\sigma_b$ parameterizes the variance in the $s(p)$ equation. A high $\sigma_b$ (e.g. from atmospheric interference or high L2A variance) flattens the penalty curve, yielding higher baseline support values even when absolute residual is moderate. This acknowledges that the *constraint* is loose because the measurement itself is uncertain. 

## 5. Numerical Sanity Checks
Executing on our real baseline unmocked L2A crop ($y$) and true SEN2SR prediction ($\hat{y}$) mapped over a `4x` factor yields stable behavior:

| Band | Residual ($e$) Max Abs | Support ($s(p)$) Min | Support ($s(p)$) Max |

| **B02** | 0.0063 | 0.9876 | 1.0000 |

| **B04** | 0.0057 | 0.9900 | 1.0000 |

*Note: As expected for an uncalibrated highly-smooth cropped area evaluated against $\sigma_b=0.04$, the support limits are uniformly high. The output map successfully shapes as `(4, 1024, 1024)` matching the HR bounds perfectly.*

## 6. Testing & Invalid Data
- **Bounds Checking**: Deterministic tests ensure that $0.0 \le s(p) \le 1.0$ inherently without arithmetic underflow failures.
- **NaN / NoData Isolation**: Tests confirm that if a region of $y$ contains `NaN` or `NoData`, its computed support $s(p)$ strictly evaluates to $0.0$. There can be no observational constraint derived from an invalid observation.
- **Full Test Suite Validation**: 12/12 pytest cases succeed, ensuring preservation of Track A, Track B, UI, and inference pathways.

## 7. Limitations
- Support is currently uniformly broadcasted across the 4x4 internal grid block. Edge boundaries across 2.5m pixels spanning 10m borders will have hard block transitions rather than sub-pixel graded transitions (which physically mirrors the true L2A sensor pixel footprints).
